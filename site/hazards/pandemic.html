Pandemic & outbreaks

The same two-week readiness covers a bad flu season, a measles cluster, a boil order, and the rare true pandemic.

Add this to my plan

No one can predict the next pandemic. Most years, the real threats to a Utah household are ordinary: a hard flu season, a measles exposure at school, West Nile in August, or a stomach bug that takes out the whole house. Being able to stay home for two weeks by choice handles all of them.

Why it matters in Utah

  • Utah has had its largest measles outbreak in decades: 724 Utah residents diagnosed since June 2025 (197 in 2025, 527 in 2026), with no new cases in the three weeks before October 20, 2026. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab)
  • H5N1 bird flu: 72 U.S. human cases since 2024 and 2 deaths, with no person-to-person spread; CDC rates the general public's risk as low (as of October 15, 2026). Utah has had no human cases. CDC (opens source in a new tab) Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab)
  • West Nile virus: 50 Utah human cases reported as of October 3, 2026, versus 49 at the same point in 2025 and 13 in 2024. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab)
  • Plague is real in Utah and rare: Utah DHHS calls it endemic in Utah wildlife. Bubonic plague responds to antibiotics; the danger is delay. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab)
  • Hantavirus is rare (about 1 to 2 Utah cases a year) but serious. It spreads from deer-mouse droppings in cabins, sheds, and garages. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab) CDC (opens source in a new tab)
  • Influenza pandemics struck in 1918, 1957, 1968, and 2009, followed by COVID-19 in 2020. That's five in about a century. This is history, not a forecast.

Who's most affected

  • Infants, unvaccinated children, pregnant people, older adults, and people with heart or lung conditions or weakened immune systems.
  • Large households. Utah's average household is the largest of any state, so illness can pass person to person for longer.
  • People who rely on daily prescriptions, especially controlled substances, which are excluded from Utah's pharmacist emergency-refill rule. Utah Code (opens source in a new tab)

What to do

1 Before

  • Build a two-week "stay home by choice" supply: food you eat, water, medicines, and hygiene items. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Keep a one-page medication list and refill on time. Ask your insurer about 90-day fills or mail order. Utah Code (opens source in a new tab)
  • Know your family's vaccination records. CDC says two MMR doses are 97% effective against measles. CDC (opens source in a new tab)
  • Stock a few NIOSH-approved N95s per person. Check the approval number on the NIOSH list; KN95s are not NIOSH-approved. NIOSH (opens source in a new tab)
  • Plan a sick room and how you'd care for two sick adults at once.
  • Avoid raw (unpasteurized) milk, don't handle sick or dead birds, and practice biosecurity with backyard flocks. CDC (opens source in a new tab)
  • Mosquito season (about July through September): use EPA-registered repellent and drain standing water. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab)
  • Cabin or shed opening: don't sweep or vacuum mouse droppings. Air it out, wet droppings with disinfectant, and wipe up with gloves. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab)

2 During

  • Follow the readiness level that fits what you see (table below). Use official sources: Utah DHHS, your local health department, and CDC. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab)
  • Isolate whoever is sick, keep shared surfaces clean, and use your sick-room supplies.
  • Use pickup and delivery to cut trips when illness is widespread.
  • Check on older neighbors and people who live alone. Keep it low-contact if needed.

3 After

  • Waves can come back. The 1918 fall wave was deadlier than the spring wave, so restock between waves. CDC (opens source in a new tab)
  • Refill prescriptions and rotate supplies you used.
  • Note what ran out first and add it to your list.

What history tells us

  • 1918: about 675,000 U.S. deaths. CDC (opens source in a new tab) Utah lost at least 2,915 people Deseret News (opens source in a new tab) [verify against the Utah Historical Quarterly]. On October 9, 1918, authorities closed Salt Lake City churches, schools, theaters, and public meetings; churches and theaters reopened December 6. Influenza Encyclopedia (opens source in a new tab)
  • 2009 H1N1: CDC estimated 12,469 U.S. deaths, April 2009 to April 2010 (range 8,868 to 18,306). Shrestha et al. 2011 (opens source in a new tab)
  • COVID-19: Utah DHHS reported 5,455 Utah deaths and 44,324 hospitalizations through November 2, 2023. Utah DHHS (opens source in a new tab) A CDC/NCHS count of death certificates mentioning COVID shows 6,362 Utah deaths through October 2026. The two count different things over different periods. CDC/NCHS (opens source in a new tab) The federal public health emergency ran from January 31, 2020 to May 11, 2023. HHS (opens source in a new tab)
  • In 1918, cities that acted early with several measures and kept them in place had lower peak death rates (Hatchett et al.). How well that applies to modern mandates is debated. Hatchett et al. 2007 (opens source in a new tab) The household lesson isn't debated: supplies before a wave let you choose when to go out.

Emergency powers in Utah today (plain language)

  • A governor's state of emergency ends after 30 days unless the Legislature extends it, and the Legislature can end it at any time. Utah Code (opens source in a new tab)
  • A state public health emergency likewise expires after 30 days unless the Legislature extends it. Utah Code (opens source in a new tab)
  • SB 195 (2021) added limits on long emergencies and on local health-department orders: a local health department must notify the county executive before an "order of constraint," and the county governing body can end it. Utah Code (opens source in a new tab) [confirm current section numbers after the 2023 recodification]
  • What this means for you: in a long outbreak, Utah is likely to rely more on short, legislature-approved orders and on voluntary household choices. Your own readiness is what lets you make those choices freely.

Headlines about mystery viruses

In October 2026, a worker at an anti-plague research institute in Irkutsk, Siberia, died of severe pneumonia. WHO reported no evidence of ongoing spread and said the cause remains undiagnosed; claims of a new virus are unconfirmed. Risk to Utah: very low. WHO (opens source in a new tab) Wait for WHO, CDC, or Utah DHHS confirmation, and keep your everyday 14-day readiness.
