Winter inversions & air quality

Inversion season comes every winter. Plan for it like the weather.

Add this to my plan

In winter, cold air gets trapped in Utah's mountain valleys under a lid of warmer air, and pollution builds up near the ground. It's predictable, so you can plan: check the forecast, adjust outdoor activity, clean your indoor air, and drive and burn less on bad days.

Why it matters in Utah

  • A typical Utah winter sees about five to six multi-day inversion episodes, and on average 18 days with fine-particle (PM2.5) levels above the national health standard. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)
  • The Wasatch and Oquirrh Mountains and Traverse Mountain form a basin that traps cold air in the Salt Lake Valley. Inversions often follow a snowstorm and may need a strong storm to clear. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)
  • Most of Utah's winter PM2.5 forms through chemical reactions in the air from emissions (vehicles, wood burning, area sources, industry). Ammonium nitrate can make up to 70% of particle mass during inversions. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)
  • Summer brings a different problem: ground-level ozone along the northern Wasatch Front. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)

Who's most affected

  • People with asthma, COPD, heart disease, or other lung conditions; older adults; children; and pregnant people.
  • People who work or exercise outdoors in the valleys during inversions.
  • Households in valley-floor neighborhoods, where inversions are strongest. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)

What to do

1 Before

  • Bookmark air.utah.gov and check the multi-day forecast and burn status each winter morning. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab) Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)
  • Change your furnace filter; use MERV 13 or higher if your system allows. EPA (opens source in a new tab)
  • Consider a portable HEPA air cleaner for a bedroom, especially for anyone with lung or heart conditions. EPA (opens source in a new tab)
  • If anyone has asthma or COPD, ask your clinician for an action plan for bad-air days.

2 During

  • On unhealthy-air days, check the action level and move hard exercise indoors, especially for sensitive groups. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab) AirNow (opens source in a new tab)
  • Follow mandatory no-burn days for wood and coal stoves (status is posted daily). Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)
  • Combine trips, carpool, or take transit; vehicle emissions are one of the sources that build up during inversions. Utah DEQ (opens source in a new tab)
  • Keep windows closed and run your air cleaner.

3 After

  • Air out your home when a storm clears the valley.
  • Replace filters that look dark or dirty.
