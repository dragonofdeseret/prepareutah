Family communication plan

When phones are jammed and you're not together, a plan on paper is how everyone finds each other.

Fill this in once, print wallet cards for everyone, and practice it. A text often gets through when a call won't, and an out-of-area contact may be easier to reach than someone across town. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)

How to use it

  • Agree on a familiar meeting place, and practice your plan with everyone at home. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Choose an out-of-area contact everyone checks in with; long-distance calls may connect when local lines are jammed. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Text instead of calling: texts use far less bandwidth. Practice short messages like "I'm OK. At the library." Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab) Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab) FEMA (opens source in a new tab)
  • Save at least one contact in every phone under "ICE" (in case of emergency). Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Keep copies in go-bags, cars, and every family member's bag, and review the plan at least once a year. Utah DEM (opens source in a new tab) Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)

Prefer a paper form?

  • Ready.gov's fillable family communication plan and Utah's Family Emergency Plan Worksheet cover the same ground, including school, work, and child-care plans. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab) Utah DEM (opens source in a new tab)
