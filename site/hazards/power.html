Power outages & winter storms

Power touches almost everything: heat, water, medicine, money. A little backup goes a long way.

Add this to my plan

Outages come from winter storms, wind, wildfire shutoffs, earthquakes, and equipment failures. Short ones are an inconvenience; long ones in a Utah winter are a safety issue, especially for people who rely on powered medical equipment.

Why it matters in Utah

  • Power outages can affect your safety, food, medication and medical equipment, access to money and gas, heat and light, and even your water supply. Be Ready Utah (opens source in a new tab)
  • Winter storms raise the risk of car accidents, hypothermia, frostbite, carbon monoxide poisoning, and heart attacks from overexertion. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Rocky Mountain Power used Public Safety Power Shutoffs in southern Utah during extreme fire conditions in June 2026. Rocky Mountain Power (opens source in a new tab)
  • A refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours if unopened; a full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)

Who's most affected

  • Anyone who uses oxygen concentrators, CPAP or BiPAP, power wheelchairs, home dialysis, or refrigerated medicines. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab) Rocky Mountain Power (opens source in a new tab)
  • Older adults, infants, and anyone vulnerable to cold. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Mobile and manufactured homes and rural or canyon homes, which may wait longer for restoration.

What to do

1 Before

  • Talk to your medical provider about a power-outage plan for powered devices and refrigerated medicines. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • If someone relies on powered medical equipment, ask Rocky Mountain Power about its Medical Certificate program (a medical professional must complete the application). Rocky Mountain Power (opens source in a new tab)
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms with battery backup, and test smoke alarms. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Keep flashlights for every household member, plus power banks and a battery or hand-crank radio. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab) Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Winterize your car and keep an emergency kit in it, and check road conditions before you go. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • If you buy a generator, plan to run it only outdoors; connect it to home wiring only through a transfer switch installed by a licensed electrician. Be Ready Utah (opens source in a new tab)

2 During

  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Never use a generator, grill, camp stove, or charcoal indoors or in a garage. Run generators outdoors at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents. CDC (opens source in a new tab) Be Ready Utah (opens source in a new tab)
  • Don't heat your home with a gas oven or stove. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Layer clothing, gather in one room, and hang blankets over windows and doors. If it gets dangerously cold, go to a warming location. Be Ready Utah (opens source in a new tab) Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Stay far from downed lines. Report them to Rocky Mountain Power at 1-888-221-7070, or call 911. Be Ready Utah (opens source in a new tab)
  • Protect pipes: insulate them and let faucets drip in freezing weather. Be Ready Utah (opens source in a new tab)

3 After

  • When in doubt, throw it out: discard food exposed to 40°F or higher for two hours or more. Ready.gov (opens source in a new tab)
  • Turn your porch light on when power returns; crews patrol to see which lights are on. Be Ready Utah (opens source in a new tab)
  • Restock batteries and fuel; note what you missed most.
